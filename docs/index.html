Alerts

Default rules (you can treat these as a starting point): Red if the 1-month change is +10% or more, or the 12-month change is +15% or more. Amber if the 3-month change is +8% or more, or the price has bounced sharply from a much lower year-ago level. Green otherwise. El Niño is added as a watch item, not a price trigger.

What we are not forecasting

  • Thai coconut and pineapple farm-gate prices have no public futures market. A simple trend or seasonal path would be a sample method only, so it is not drawn as a prediction here. The El Niño section below explains the historical pattern to watch, especially for coconut into 2027.
  • Thai white sugar is an administered domestic price (last published 21 baht/kg white and 22 baht/kg refined, effective 8 November 2025 for crop year 2568/69). It does not follow the ICE curve unless the Office of the Cane and Sugar Board issues a new notice. Confirm whether this is still in force for 2569/70.
  • THB/USD: no official 3–6 month BOT forecast is shown. A weaker baht (more baht per dollar) makes imported inputs dearer in baht terms.

El Niño watch — why it matters for Malee’s crops

A very strong El Niño is under way in the tropical Pacific. In plain language: the eastern Pacific is much warmer than usual, which often raises the chance of drier-than-normal weather in parts of Thailand. That can cut yields of rain-fed sugarcane, coconut and pineapple, and can lift farm prices later. It is a tendency, not a guarantee.

What this usually means for Thai crops — and what to watch now

CropTypical weather linkWhat the record shows, and what to watch
Sugarcane El Niño often raises the chance of below-normal rain and heat in rain-fed areas, especially the Northeast (about 44% of cane area). Drought during the growing season can cut cane yield and the amount of sugar extracted from each tonne. The Thai Meteorological Department’s 3-month outlook for October–December 2026 (issued 25 Sep 2026) expected rainfall below normal in most of the country, and about 60% below normal in the Northeast. USDA FAS, citing TMD rainfall for 1 Jan–30 Sep 2026, forecasts 2026/27 cane at 95 million tonnes, down 10% from 105.9 million, and sugar at 9.5 million tonnes, down 21%, partly on El Niño. World raw sugar has eased in the past month, but Thai crop risk is still a watch item for 2027 supply. TMD Oct–Dec 2026 outlook (PDF) · TMD ENSO desk · USDA FAS Sugar Semi-annual TH2026-0038
Coconut Coconut palms are long-lived. Drought and heat can reduce nut set, with the effect on harvests and farm prices often showing up many months later — not in the same month as the peak ocean temperature. After the very strong 2015–16 El Niño, the official OAE farm-gate price for large mature coconut peaked at ฿18.82/nut in February 2017. After the 2023–24 El Niño, it peaked at ฿22.36/nut in December 2024. The official October 2026 price is ฿8.27/nut — still far below a year ago (−53.9%), though it has bounced from the August low of ฿6.02. If that 12–18 month lag were to repeat, 2027 is the year to watch. This is a historical pattern from official prices, not a forecast. There is no official national monthly series for aromatic nam-hom coconut. OAE coconut farm-gate prices · NOAA ONI
Pineapple and other fruit Pineapple is relatively shallow-rooted and can suffer in a hot, dry spell, especially if young plants wilt. Lower harvests have in past dry episodes been followed by firmer farm-gate and factory prices. Trade commentary on the 2023 El Niño described stunted Thai winter pineapple and a tighter 2023 crop. Official OAE factory-pineapple prices today are ฿9.71/kg in October 2026, up 11.5% over three months and 4.2% versus a year ago — already an amber watch, and worth tracking if TMD’s drier outlook holds. OAE pineapple farm-gate prices · CZ, Thai pineapple and the 2023 El Niño (trade report, not an official statistic)

A Thai bank research note (Krungthai COMPASS, reported by The Nation) has estimated large farm-income losses in a severe 2026–27 El Niño drought scenario, with sugarcane among the affected crops. That is a private scenario, not a government forecast. The Nation, 2026. La Niña, when it occurs, more often raises the chance of heavier rain and flood damage in parts of Thailand — the opposite weather risk, and not the current official status.

What the record says, in plain English

  • Thai sugar output fell in the crop years of every major El Niño shown: 1997/98 −29%, 2009/10 −4%, 2015/16 −14% (vs 2013/14), 2023/24 −20%, and USDA forecasts 2026/27 at 9.5 Mt, −16% vs 2025/26. The 2009/10 dip was small and was followed by a record rebound — the size of the hit varies.
  • World sugar prices did not rise every time. Over 12 months after each El Niño onset since 1995, the median change in the World Bank world sugar price was -7.3% (4 of 9 episodes rose), versus -3.8% for all 12-month windows. Brazil’s crop and oil prices often matter more for the world price.
  • Thai coconut is the clearest lagged pattern. The OAE farm-gate price peaked in Feb 2017 (after 2015–16) and Dec 2024 (after 2023–24), 12–18 months after the ocean peak. That is why 2027 is flagged as the year to watch.
  • Coconut oil’s 2009–11 jump happened during a broad commodity boom, so not all of it is El Niño.

What a cost move could mean for gross margin

Sample weights — not Malee’s actual cost mix. Move the sliders to try different shares of cost of sales. The starting margin is the public 9M 2026 figure of 17.4%. The method is simple: if an item is W% of cost of sales, and its price rises by P%, gross margin falls by about W% × P% × 82.6% (because cost of sales was 82.6% of sales at a 17.4% margin).
